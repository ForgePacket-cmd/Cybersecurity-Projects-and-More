# My First Steps into Hack The Box

I've recently started using Hack The Box as a way to develop my practical cybersecurity skills.

My first machine was a bit of a learning curve. I'll be honest going between a write up and a video walkthrough just to complete one machine took me more hours then I'd like to admit, but completing it gave me a much better understanding of how the platform works and what to expect from these challenges.

After that, I moved on to Nibbles. I started exploring directory enumeration and researching the different stages of the machine, If I'm honest it felt like I bit off more than I could chew.

Rather than spend too much time struggling without a solid foundation, I decided to take a step back and look for a more structured way to learn. And frankly there must've been a simpler way to do it then the way I was going.

That's when I went back to Hack The Box's Starting Point section and worked through the first couple recommended machines.
This turned out to be a really worthwhile decision. The machines introduced me to a range of fundamental concepts and the write ups explained the code well. It helped me become more comfortable with the tools and gave me a better understanding of how to approach these challenges methodically. For instance I didn't know that most of it was just enumeration and poking around and trying things, I mean hey I wasn't complaining it helped me understand more of how a hacker thinks. We all see in the movies some guy in a chair furiously typing on a machine and then there's just an ACCESS GRANTED sign that shows up in big text and then they cut to the next scene. I've done my research online but most of it is just trial and error until you find a way in and then you privilege escalate until you're admin and BOOM you've got the keys to the kingdom, what do you want to do with it? The choice is yours.

I get that it’s more complex than that so here's the method I found:

1. Reconnaissance (Information Gathering)
2. Scanning and Enumeration
3. Gaining Access (Exploitation)
4. Maintaining Access and Privilege Escalation (Post-Exploitation)
   
Listen, I don't know everything and I haven't been playing around with computers and code since I was 12 compared to some people I know. But I don't have to. Lets be honest all they’ve got on me is experience and knowledge, 2 things I can achieve over time. 

So moving forward I’ll document as best I can and see where it goes.
